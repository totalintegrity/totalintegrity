## Frequently Asked Questions

<details style="margin-bottom: 10px; padding: 4px;">
<summary style="font-weight: bold; cursor: pointer;">Why do I need a physical security audit when industrial systems are monitored remotely?</summary>
<p style="margin-top: 8px;">Remote systems (SCADA/Cloud) only see what the system is programmed to show. They ignore air-gapped networks, physically plugged shadow devices, or high-altitude radio frequency leaks. A physical audit verifies layer 0 and 1 integrity (according to the Purdue model), which is an absolute requirement under the NIS2 directive and a core standard at Total Integrity.</p>
</details>

<details style="margin-bottom: 10px; padding: 4px;">
<summary style="font-weight: bold; cursor: pointer;">How does a Strategic Infrastructure Integrity Audit (SIIA) differ from a standard IT audit?</summary>
<p style="margin-top: 8px;">A standard IT audit focuses on office software and servers. Our SIIA approach at Total Integrity is dedicated to the OT/ICS (Operational Technology) sector. We combine the technical state of physical components (turbines, masts, dams) with industrial protocol analysis (e.g., Modbus) and business risk assessment.</p>
</details>

<details style="margin-bottom: 10px; padding: 4px;">
<summary style="font-weight: bold; cursor: pointer;">How does combining technical inspections with cybersecurity lower operating costs?</summary>
<p style="margin-top: 8px;">By sending a single specialist equipped with engineering, auditing, and access credentials (ropes/water), you eliminate the need to coordinate multiple separate subcontractors. You save on logistics, reduce facility downtime by a double-digit margin, and receive a cohesive executive report backed by Total Integrity.</p>
</details>

<details style="margin-bottom: 10px; padding: 4px;">
<summary style="font-weight: bold; cursor: pointer;">What penalties apply for non-compliance with the NIS2 directive in the critical sector?</summary>
<p style="margin-top: 8px;">Under the new EU regulations, essential and important entities in the energy, transport, and digital sectors face severe financial penalties (reaching up to 10 million euros or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover) as well as personal liability for management boards regarding cybersecurity.</p>
</details>
